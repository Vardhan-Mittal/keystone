Keystone

Every dependency scanner looks for broken packages.
This one looks for the people.

A package is only as trustworthy as whoever can publish it. Keystone loads a real application's dependency tree into a graph, adds the humans who hold publish rights, and walks the graph to answer one question: who can reach your code, and how?

Why counting packages gets the answer wrong

The left dot is how many packages an account can publish. The right dot is how much of the tree that actually reaches. The gap is the dependency graph doing the work — and it reorders the ranking.

Ask the graph

A Gemini agent picks from eight graph tools, runs them against FalkorDB, and chains them until it can answer. Every step shows the Cypher it ran, so you can re-run it yourself.

What this is not. A high reach number describes where someone sits in a dependency graph — not whether they can be trusted. Several of the accounts ranked here are themselves past victims of phishing. Concentration is a property of the ecosystem, not a judgement about a person.
